## 0.7.0

**Before you upgrade:** every Toposcope page now refuses to be framed (`frame-ancestors 'none'`): a page that put Toposcope in an iframe stops showing it, and should embed a share's panel instead. Nothing changes in ClickHouse; SQLite gains a `shares` table that 0.6.3 leaves unread on a rollback.

Another app can read Toposcope's numbers without the password. A share is a scope of data and a secret: metric names (exact or `fleet_*`), label matchers such as `service:agental`, a log `q`, and the keys a read may split by or narrow by. Toposcope adds the scope to every read made with the share, on the server, so one share feeds every panel a page needs and the page never states it. Reads are aggregates only: `GET /share/v1/series` (one metric series, or a log count or rate), `GET /share/v1/top` (Top-N counts by a listed key) and `GET /share/v1/names` (what a picker needs), with `Authorization: Bearer <secret>`; events, traces, fingerprints and everything under `/api` never answer a share, and a metric outside the scope reads as one that does not exist. Signed in, `POST /api/shares` makes one and returns its secret once, `PUT /api/shares/<name>` makes or changes one, `DELETE` revokes it at once. See `docs/shares.md`. [#225](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/225)

A share has limits, so a holder cannot pull a fine-grained copy of its scope. Each share has a finest bar (`min_step`, default `1m`: Auto snaps up to it, an explicit finer `step=` is refused and says so), a longest window (`max_range`, default `7d`), an oldest reach (`max_lookback`, default `30d`, so a week at a time cannot walk back through history) and a rate (`reads_per_minute`, default 120, then `429` with `Retry-After`); `PUT /api/shares/<name>` takes them as `limits`. What a share was used for is a series: once a minute each share that was read writes a `service:toposcope share:<name> status:used` row with its reads, refusals, rate-limited reads and the page origins it was read from, and every refused read writes a row with its reason, so a monitor can page on a share being misused. No viewer addresses are kept. `GET /api/shares` gives each share's last read and its reads and refusals over 24 hours. [#226](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/226)

A share's panels can be embedded in another app, as frames 23a–23f and 23l draw them. The host's backend trades the share's secret for a ticket (`POST /share/v1/tickets`; an hour by default, at most a day, never past the share) and puts the ticket in the page, never the secret; a ticket reads what its share reads, mints nothing and dies with the share. A `<script src=".../embed.js" data-ticket=… data-metric=…>` tag places an iframe of `/embed`: one panel, nothing that leads back into Toposcope, light or dark, kept and muted with its last read time when Toposcope stops answering, saying so when its ticket expires or the share refuses a param, and polling at its bar width but never faster than every 30 seconds. When Toposcope cannot be reached at all, the tag puts one plain *Toposcope unreachable* line in its place for the host's CSS. Each share lists the origins that may frame it (`embed.frame_ancestors`, no `*`; none means JSON only), and every other Toposcope page now refuses to be framed. Answers are kept until the panel's next bar or 30 seconds, so many viewers of one panel cost one read and do not count against the rate. [#227](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/227)

Shares are made, read and revoked in the app: Settings → Shares, as frames 23g–23j draw it, lists each share's scope and limits in a line, when it was last read and its reads and refusals over 24 hours (refusals open its `share:<name>` rows as a hunt), makes one with a live count of the metric names it takes in, shows its secret once with the ticket call and the script tag, and confirms a revoke in the row. A metric panel's copy menu (its ⋯ when narrow) gains **Copy embed**, frame 23k: the panel's script tag through the first share that covers it, or why none does. [#228](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/228)

## 0.6.1

A monitor can check a port and a certificate, not only a URL. A TCP monitor connects to `host:port` from this node every 30s to 1h, within 10s, and sends nothing; its lines are `connected` and `time < 200ms`. A certificate monitor does a TLS handshake there and reads the certificate, from the first byte or after STARTTLS on SMTP, IMAP or POP3; its lines are `cert > 14d` and `cert name == mail.example.com` (the certificate is for that name, wildcards as a client reads them), and `connected` and `time` too. A certificate this node would not trust fails, and so does one not for its host unless a `cert name` line says which it must be. Both run on the HTTP(S) monitors' clock and are recorded, opened and resolved as they are: each run a log row (`monitor:<name> status:<status> source:tcp|cert`, with `target`, `duration_ms`, the line and the error, and the certificate's subject and days left), 2 failed runs in a row to open, the next passing run to resolve. New monitor gains the TCP · certificate card; its editor connects once from this node and shows how it answered (`connected · TLS 1.3 via STARTTLS`), where the time went and the certificate (when it expires, its name, its issuer), a chip clicked becoming a line. Watch filters TCP and Certificate; a lane reads `tcp · 30s · connected` or `certificate · SMTP · 1h`, and a certificate lane its days left. `PUT /api/monitors/<name>` takes `"source": "tcp"` or `"cert"` with `target` and `starttls`. ICMP, DNS and UDP stay out; anything else is a push monitor. On a hunt, an HTTP(S) monitor's stretch now says what failed it instead of "at least 1 line". [#180](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/180)

Upgrading is one command on the host, with a backup first and a way back. Every release ships `upgrade.sh`, an operator script of its own, apart from the agent skill's `toposcope.sh`. `./upgrade.sh [X.Y.Z] [--no-backup]` checks the release's files against its `SHA256SUMS`, prints each newer release's Before you upgrade, pulls the new image while the old one runs, backs up both volumes into `backups/` (keeping the last 3, saying where and how big), swaps `compose.yml` (keeping `.env`, `compose.override.yml` and an edited `vector.yaml`), waits for the new version, and posts a deploy mark. When the new version does not come up it starts the old one again, and restores the backup if the old one cannot open its data either. `./upgrade.sh backup` takes the backup alone; `./upgrade.sh rollback --yes` goes back to the last one. `docs/operations.md` starts its upgrade section with the command and gains What Toposcope calls out to, with the recipe for paging on a new release. [#198](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/198), [#199](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/199)

A release's files can be checked before they are used. Every release now attaches `SHA256SUMS`, one line per file in `sha256sum` format, and `toposcope.sh` is one of its files, so `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS` checks a download of all five. `toposcope.sh up` checks each file it downloads against the release's `SHA256SUMS` before it puts it in place: a file that does not match, or one the sums do not list, starts nothing and is named, and a release with no `SHA256SUMS` starts nothing either. [#195](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/195)

The footer says which Toposcope is running, and that a newer one is out. The running version sits at its right end; when the release check knows of a newer release it reads `0.6.0 → 0.6.1`, with an amber dot when a release in between has a Before you upgrade. Clicking it opens a popover, as frames 19b–19i draw it: Before you upgrade first, then what's new (each release's notes, collapsed when several are out), then the one command to run on the host, `./upgrade.sh 0.6.1`, with a copy button, and how big the backup it takes first will be, from the stores' own sizes. A check that failed or is off is said in the popover, never in the footer, and never as up to date. `/api/version` gains `store` (ClickHouse's active parts and the SQLite file) while a newer release is known. [#201](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/201)

A new release can page someone. The first time the release check sees a version newer than the one running, Toposcope writes one log row for it, `service:toposcope event:update_available`, at level info, with `running`, `latest`, `step` (patch, minor or major), `behind` (how many releases lie between) and the release link. A saved search for it with a monitor at **at least 1** pages through that monitor's webhook or the Settings default; `step:minor OR step:major` pages only on the bigger steps. One row per version: a restart writes none, a newer release writes one more, and a check that fails or is off writes nothing. [#197](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/197)

Toposcope knows when a newer release is out. Once a day, and a few minutes after boot, it reads GitHub's public list of releases and keeps every stable release newer than the one running: version, date, link, notes, and the release's own Before you upgrade. It sends nothing about the instance. A check that fails keeps its last answer and says why, quietly. `GET /api/version`, signed in, gives the running version, the latest, the step (patch, minor or major), and every release between them, newest first. `/api/health` is unchanged. `TOPOSCOPE_UPDATE_CHECK=off` in `.env` turns the check off, and then nothing is called; Compose and the env example pass it through. A CHANGELOG version section may now carry one paragraph that starts **Before you upgrade:**, which the release body and `/api/version` carry apart from the notes. [#196](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/196)

**Before you upgrade:** From this version Toposcope asks GitHub once a day whether a newer release is out. It sends nothing about this instance. Set `TOPOSCOPE_UPDATE_CHECK=off` in `.env` to stop it. From this version on, `upgrade.sh` backs up both stores before it upgrades, unless given `--no-backup`, and ingest stops while the backup runs: a few seconds for a small store, minutes for a store of gigabytes.

A minted link key no longer widens its dialog. After Mint key, the direction strip, the filter, the key and their Copy buttons ran out past the right edge of the 480px dialog, because the dialog kept room for the whole key on one line. The key is now cut in its field, as frame 7a draws it, and Copy still takes all of it. Every dialog now stays as wide as it is drawn, whatever unbroken text it holds. [#194](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/194)

## 0.6.0

A monitor can call a URL. An HTTP(S) monitor runs from the Toposcope node every 30s to 1h, on its own clock: GET, HEAD, or POST with a body, with headers kept as a webhook address is (sent, never shown back), and a 10s timeout. It passes when all its conditions pass, one a line: `status == 200`, `status in 200..299`, `time < 300ms`, `body contains "ok"`, `$.db == "ok"` or `$.queue.depth < 100` (a restricted JSONPath, no filters or scripts), `header content-type contains json`, `cert > 14d`; with none, any 2xx passes, and a line marked warn warns instead of failing. A redirect is not followed and fails the run, and so does a certificate that is not trusted for the host. It opens after 2 failed runs in a row (settable) and resolves on the next passing run, with the same reminders and resolved call as any monitor; without a webhook of its own it calls the default one in Settings. Every run is a log row (`monitor:<name> status:<status> source:http`, with `http.status`, `duration_ms`, the line that decided it and the error), and `duration_ms` is also the metric `monitor_duration_ms`. The editor sends the request once from the node and shows the response, where its time went and its certificate; a value clicked becomes a condition, stored as lines, and it says what the last response would be with them. On Watch its lane draws its runs, a warn amber and a fail red; Watch keeps the worst run of each minute for 30 days. [#178](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/178)

A script, a cron job or a box with no logs can tell Toposcope how it went. `POST /v1/checks` takes one result or up to 500 with the ingest token: `ok`, `warn` or `fail`, or Nagios's `0`–`3`, with a message, a value and its unit, and when the next one is due (`every`, `grace`). A name not seen before creates a push monitor, up to 500. For a cron line with no token, each push monitor has a keyed URL, `/ping/<key>`, that takes a GET or a POST as Healthchecks' does (`/start`, `/fail`, `/<exit code>`; a POST body up to 10 KB is the message); an unknown key gets 404 and writes nothing, a key stores one start and one result a second, and the key is printed only on its monitor's page. A push monitor is late past `every` with nothing in, and opens past `every` plus its grace or on a fail; the next ok resolves it, a warn shows and calls nobody, and unknown changes nothing. Every result is a log row (`monitor:<name> status:<status> source:push`, with its exit code, and how long its run took from its `/start`), and a value is the metric `monitor_value`. An attached `/v1/probes` check is read as a result too. `toposcope.sh check <monitor> -- <command>` runs any command or Nagios plugin and sends its result. Watch draws a push monitor's results on the clock, its runs as bars and late in amber, and the header counts the late ones; New monitor has a Push card, and a push monitor's editor shows how to send results and the first one that arrived. A monitor without a webhook of its own calls the new Settings → Default webhook, and with none it keeps its state and calls nobody. A hunt reads its monitors' stretches again once its search answers, not only at the next 30s read. [#177](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/177)

A monitor can say a stream went quiet. A monitor on a saved search now opens on **at least** or **fewer than** its threshold: `host:ci-box` over 15m, fewer than 1, opens when no line has come from the box for 15 minutes, and the next line resolves it. Fewer than works on a count or a rate (no lines is a rate of 0); a p99 or an average over no lines has no value, so fewer than refuses them with that reason, and it refuses a saved search with a fixed from/to. It stays shut while it can't tell, and Watch says which: a refused or failed reading, the first window after Toposcope starts, or a linked source that could feed the search is behind or quiet, so the count is a floor. Watch and the editor show when the search last matched a line ("last line 10:26:41, 37m ago"), and the editor reads as 18j draws it. `PUT /api/monitors/<name>` takes `"opens_when": "fewer_than"`. The editor's Send a test call now sends the resolved body too when there is one, as it says. [#176](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/176)

Something now notices when Toposcope itself stops. ## 0.5.2

OTLP over gRPC. Several OpenTelemetry exporters send gRPC unless told otherwise, and Toposcope took OTLP over HTTP only, so those apps failed to connect until someone found the protocol setting. A second listener, on port `4319`, takes the Export call of the logs, trace, metrics and profiles services, with the same ingest token as `authorization` metadata. Each call is handed to the HTTP route for its signal, so the 1 MB and 1,024-record caps, gzip and the partial-success reply are the same; a refusal comes back as `INVALID_ARGUMENT`, `UNAUTHENTICATED` or `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED`, and busy or stopping as `UNAVAILABLE`, which an exporter retries. The port is not OTLP’s usual `4317`, which is left to a collector on the same host such as the packaged Vector config. `OTLP_GRPC_PORT` moves it and `0` turns it off; the packaged install binds it to `127.0.0.1`. It speaks HTTP/2 without TLS, so behind a reverse proxy it needs a rule of its own. [#75](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/75)

A span keeps the exception recorded on it. Tracing libraries record an exception as an event on the span, with its type, message and stack; Toposcope stored the span and dropped its events, so the waterfall showed a failed span and not what was thrown. The span now keeps `exception.type`, `exception.message` and `exception.stacktrace`, and the `e1` those give, worked out by the code that fingerprints a log row: a selected span shows them in the waterfall, and the id is the one on the log row for the same exception. It does not become an error row. The same failure is recorded on every span it passes through and is usually logged as well, so fingerprints, `level:error` and Compare keep reading logged errors, and an exception still has to be logged to be hunted. Spans already stored keep what they have. [#73](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/73)

The packaged `vector.yaml` is a real collector: Vector listens for OTLP from apps, over HTTP on 4318 and gRPC on 4317, keeps what it receives in a disk buffer, and forwards logs, traces and metrics to Toposcope as the app sent them. It was a smoke source only, and showed none of that. An app that speaks OpenTelemetry straight to Toposcope loses what it emits while Toposcope is down for longer than its exporter keeps retrying; with Vector in between the app changes one address and everything it sent arrives once Toposcope is back. Vector stays optional, and the release zip is still four files: the smoke source is in the same file and still sends three events on every start. Running it now needs somewhere to keep its buffers (`VECTOR_DATA_DIR`, `/var/lib/vector` when unset) and ports 4317 and 4318 free, and Vector keeps running after the smoke events. `TOPOSCOPE_URL` says where Toposcope is when it is on another host. [#76](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/76)

A histogram’s buckets are kept, so it can be read as an average or a percentile. 0.5.0 kept a histogram’s count and sum, as two counters, and dropped the buckets that hold its p99. A histogram is now one name of its own kind, `histogram`, and how it is read is chosen beside the name, where a log field’s reducer sits: `count`, `sum`, `avg`, `p50`, `p90`, `p99`. One reading is drawn at a time, and the reading is part of the link and of a saved search (`metric=p99:http.server.request.duration`). A percentile is read off the buckets of every matching series added together, never as an average of each series’ own percentile; past the highest bound it is that bound. The hover readout, a card’s title and line tag, and a Stat card’s note name the reading first, and a Stat head’s function is a choice for a histogram. `<name>.count` and `<name>.sum` fold into the one name in the Series list; a link or a saved search that carries one keeps drawing the same line, as that reading. An exponential histogram is brought to the same shape, and a summary keeps its count and sum; both were refused before. Boot adds two ClickHouse tables, `metric_buckets` and `metric_buckets_by_minute`. A histogram stored before this has no buckets, so its percentiles start with the first export after the upgrade. [#78](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/78)

The Series control is a list you can type into, on the pinned histogram, in a timeseries card’s footer, and on a Stat card’s head. It was a short native menu of the eight busiest ingested names, and an app exporting OpenTelemetry sends dozens: the rest could be drawn only by typing `metric=` into the URL. Off (Count on a card), Rate, and the numeric log fields keep the top; the eight busiest metrics follow, with a line saying how many more have points in the window. Typing any part of a name finds any metric with points in the window and any numeric log field, not only the ones listed. Each row says what it is — `field`, `gauge`, `counter` — and so does the picked control. A gauge prints `avg` beside its name and a counter prints `sum / <bar width>`; the hover readout, a card’s title and line tag, a Stat card’s head and note, and the line under the plot all name that reading first, so a counter’s sum is not read as a level. A long name is whole in the list and cut in the middle on a picked control. A picked metric with no points in the window stays picked, and the line under the plot says the line is missing, not zero. While Live is on, an open list holds still. `GET /api/metric-names` returns each name’s kind and the window’s total, and takes `find` and `picked`. [#77](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/77)

## 0.5.1

A counter or a histogram sent as a running total is converted, so a stock OpenTelemetry setup needs no setting. An exporter sends totals since the process started unless it is told otherwise, and 0.5.0 refused those. `POST /v1/metrics` now keeps the last total of each series in memory and stores the difference. A service that restarts is counted from its new start, and an export that never arrived is made up by the next one. A restart of Toposcope costs a series that was already running what it counted since its last stored export: the first total seen afterwards is only its baseline, and the reply says how many points were taken that way. The skill and the guide drop `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_METRICS_TEMPORALITY_PREFERENCE=delta`; an exporter that still sends deltas is stored as before. [#74](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/74)

## 0.5.0

On `POST /v1/logs` the logger’s name is kept. OTLP carries it as the scope’s name beside each group of records, and it was dropped; a row now has it as `otel.scope.name`, an ordinary attribute, counted toward the 50-attribute cap after the record’s own attributes and its trace and span ids. [#72](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/72)

`POST /v1/metrics` takes OTLP: JSON, protobuf, and gzip, beside the JSON point it always took. An app with a stock OpenTelemetry setup got a **400** on every metrics export. A gauge is stored as it is, and so is an up-down counter’s running total. A counter sent as deltas is stored as the amount per interval and drawn as a sum per bar, not an average. A histogram sent as deltas is stored as two counters, `<name>.count` and `<name>.sum`; buckets are not kept yet. A name remembers its kind, dotted names are kept, a point’s attributes become labels ahead of the resource’s, and a request is limited by its size, not by a count of points. A running total, an exponential histogram, and a summary are counted as rejected in the reply, with a 200, and the rest of the request is stored. The exporter needs one setting, `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_METRICS_TEMPORALITY_PREFERENCE=delta`; the skill writes it and turns the metrics exporter on. [#70](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/70)

On `POST /v1/logs` a record whose body is not a string is kept. A number or a boolean becomes its text. A map — what a collector that parses JSON lines sends, or a logger handed a dict — keeps one line as the message, its `message` or `msg` field or else the whole map as JSON, and its other top-level fields join the row’s attributes after the record’s own. A record with no body takes its event name. One with neither is still not stored, and the reply counts it. Before, every one of these was dropped with a 200. [#71](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/71)

A 200 from an OTLP route now says what did not land. `POST /v1/logs`, `/v1/traces`, and `/v1/profiles` fill OTLP’s partial success: a count of the records that were not stored and one line of why, or a count of zero and a warning when everything was stored but something was cut, such as attributes past the 50-attribute cap. The rest of the request is still stored. A protobuf request is answered in protobuf, as the protocol says, so an exporter no longer logs that it could not read the reply; a JSON reply keeps `ingested` beside the new field. A request that cannot be read keeps its 4xx. [#69](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/69)

## 0.4.12

A request that carries a valid ingest token and a wrong path under `/v1/` is answered JSON **404**, not **401**. Only the ingest routes take a bearer token, so a sender with a typo in its endpoint was told its token was wrong. A token that was never issued still gets 401 there, and an ingest token still reads nothing under `/api/`. [#84](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/84)

The app exits when it is told to stop. On SIGTERM — `docker compose stop`, an upgrade, a host shutdown — it finishes the requests in flight and the syslog queue, turns anything new away with **503**, and exits 0. Before, the signal was ignored: every stop waited out Docker’s ten-second grace period, and the app was then killed with any request that was still inserting cut off. [#83](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/83)

Errors with such a frame get a new id once at upgrade; old rows are not rewritten. [#63](https://github.com/toposcope/toposcope/issues/63)
